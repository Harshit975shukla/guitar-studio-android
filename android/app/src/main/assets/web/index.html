          Why a fifth? Why does the circle return to C?
          
    C to G spans seven semitones and five letter names: C, D, E, F, G. Repeating that pitch-class step visits all twelve positions before returning to C. Moving counterclockwise is a fifth down, equivalent in pitch class to a fourth up. The wheel is a map of keys, not guitar frets or strumming directions.
    
        
    
        
    
          Relative and parallel are different relationships
          
    C major and A natural minor share a key signature and note collection, but have different tonics. C major and C minor share the tonic C, but not the same scale notes. The key panel shows both relationships for your selection. Some theoretical parallel keys use double accidentals; an enharmonic spelling can be easier to read.
    
        
    
        
    
          Read a key signature—and understand minor-key exceptions
          
    Sharps are added in the order F, C, G, D, A, E, B; flats in the reverse order B, E, A, D, G, C, F. The signature belongs to a major key and its relative natural minor. Minor music can raise its seventh to form a major V chord: D minor keeps B♭ in its signature, while an A-major dominant uses C♯ as an accidental.
    
        
    
        
    
          Turn the circle into guitar practice
          
    Uppercase Roman numerals name major triads, lowercase names minor triads, and ° marks diminished. Hear the tonic, subdominant and dominant, then compare a cadence. These are generic harmony examples, not transcriptions of songs. Open the selected key in Scales to practise its notes; your existing range and position choices are preserved.

    Drums & tabla
    
        
    Recorded drums, tabla and hand percussion for your guitar practice: expressive grooves to play along with, or steady pulse patterns.
    
      
    
      
    
      
    Pulse patterns
    
      
    One written stroke per pulse, including recorded tabla bols and hand-drum patterns.
    
      
    
      
    Choose a pattern
    
      
    Keep its original instruments or try another sound above. Instrument changes keep the pulse order and timing; they do not turn a pattern into an authentic performance of another tradition.
    
      
    
    
    
  
    

  
  
    
    
    
      
      
    
    
    
    
      
      
      
    
    
    
    
    
    
    
    
    
    
    
    
    
      
      
      
      
    
    
    
    Choose a sound, then start a pattern.
    
    
    Volume controls only percussion; 0% mutes it and 100–150% adds a boost. Start with a comfortable device volume. Soft limiting controls percussion peaks, not the total level of every app playing on your device.
    
    
    Tabla recordings are MP3 files, with onset and level adjustments at playback. One written pulse per tempo beat; human feel never changes the cycle length. Changing kits reloads and restarts a playing pattern. Use headphones with the microphone. Stop, Escape, leaving this tool or hiding the page ends playback.

    Beats are quarter-note units. Tempo changes use zero-based beat positions. Source notes use strings 1–6 (1 is high E) and fret offsets 0–36 relative to the capo.
